# Molecule/Medicine in Numbers

One static page about pharmacology from the bench to the bedside. It starts with the approved drugs themselves: their types, drug-likeness and size by decade, the rings, atoms and stereocentres they are built from, their names, flags and therapeutic classes. Then their targets: protein classes and families, actions, the most-drugged targets and the drug–target network, the Guide to Pharmacology’s receptors, channels and enzymes, orphans and endogenous ligands, genetic support, the Davis 2011 kinome panel and the structures in the PDB and GPCRdb. Then bioactivity: the 24 million activity records in ChEMBL, potency by assay type, ligand efficiency, kinobeads against KINOMEscan, binding kinetics from K4DD, receptor theory and binding explorers, and drug pairs from NCI-ALMANAC. Then pharmacokinetics: bioavailability, half-life and protein binding of approved drugs, a dosing simulator, FDA’s CYP and transporter interaction table, and CiPA’s cardiac ion channel margins. Then trials: drug and biologic trials registered on ClinicalTrials.gov by year and phase, their design and size by phase, how soon results reach the registry, the chance and cost of getting a drug through development, and psychedelic and cannabinoid trials. Then manufacturing: drug recalls by class, cause and firm, nitrosamine recalls and FDA’s acceptable intakes, the shortage list, the Inactive Ingredient Database, and the ICH limits on residual solvents and elemental impurities. Then approvals: FDA’s novel approvals and the days they fall on, priority review and orphan drugs, FDA beside the European Medicines Agency, generics and their equivalence codes, Orange Book patents and exclusivity, and biosimilars. Hand-built SVG charts with no runtime dependencies, each with its numbers as a table and a CSV, and molecules drawn from their ChEMBL molfiles.

Lenses at the top filter the page: `?lens=basic`, `?lens=clinical`, `?lens=safety` or `?lens=genomics`.

## Run it

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run serve` | Serves the page at http://127.0.0.1:8080/ |
| `npm run lint` | ESLint, Stylelint and html-validate, each with a no-comments rule |
| `npm test` | Lint, unit tests, and Playwright end-to-end tests at desktop and phone widths |
| `npm run snapshot` | Rebuilds `src/snapshot.js` from ChEMBL, GtoPdb, the RCSB PDB, GPCRdb, ClinVar, HGNC, NCI-ALMANAC, FDA, the CiPA repository, ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA and EMA |
| `npm run snapshot -- targets network kinome` | Rebuilds only the named sections: `chembl`, `drugs`, `chemistry`, `targets`, `network`, `kinome`, `gtopdb`, `genetics`, `structures`, `activities`, `agreement`, `kinetics`, `almanac`, `pk`, `ddi`, `cipa`, `trials`, `reporting`, `psychoactive`, `recalls`, `nitrosamines`, `shortages`, `excipients`, `approvals`, `orangebook`, `ema`, `biosimilars` |

Set `SNAPSHOT_CACHE` to a folder to keep the downloaded files between runs. The end-to-end tests need a Chromium for Playwright: `npx playwright install chromium`.

## Publish it

The `Publish site` workflow deploys to `https://collide2954.github.io/molecule-medicine/` on every push to `master` and after each weekly snapshot (Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions). `.github/workflows/snapshot.yml` rebuilds the data every Sunday and commits it when numbers change. A section whose source fails keeps last week’s numbers and its own date, and the run log names it.

## Where things live

- `src/queries.js`: every API and file behind the numbers.
- `scripts/chembl.mjs`: reads ChEMBL’s release counts, approved drugs, ATC hierarchy, mechanisms of action, targets and protein classes. `scripts/gtopdb.mjs` reads the Guide to Pharmacology’s targets and interactions, `scripts/kinome.mjs` lays the Davis 2011 Kd values out by kinome group and scores selectivity, `scripts/structures.mjs` counts PDB and GPCRdb structures, `scripts/genetics.mjs` compares the genes of drug targets in ClinVar with all HGNC protein-coding genes, and `scripts/network.mjs` with `scripts/layout.mjs` builds the drug–target network and packs a force layout of each connected piece.
- `scripts/bioactivity.mjs` reads the aggregations ChEMBL’s activity search index returns, normalises kinetic rates and pairs Kd values across studies; `scripts/almanac.mjs` scores NCI-ALMANAC drug pairs; `scripts/adme.mjs` reads FDA’s interaction table, CiPA’s ion channel data and per-drug PK medians; `scripts/trials.mjs` reads ClinicalTrials.gov field counts and study records, times results submissions by sponsor and matches psychedelic and cannabinoid trials by intervention name; `scripts/manufacturing.mjs` files openFDA recalls by cause, counts nitrosamine recalls by drug, reads FDA’s nitrosamine intake tables, folds shortage listings into drugs and summarises the Inactive Ingredient Database; `scripts/approvals.mjs` reads the Drugs@FDA and Orange Book data files, EMA’s medicines report and FDA’s biosimilar list.
- `scripts/readers.mjs` reads CSV and other delimited text, xlsx workbooks, SAS transport files and HTML tables; `scripts/streams.mjs` streams, unzips and caches downloads; `scripts/sections.mjs` runs each section and keeps the last good values when one fails.
- `src/chem.js`: molfile reading and packing, implicit hydrogens, ring perception, aromaticity, ring names, ring-system keys and the bond geometry the drawings use.
- `src/findings.js`, `src/targets.js` and `src/bioactivity.js`: the counts behind each part and the section headlines, computed from the data so they stay true after each update. `src/theory.js` and `src/pk.js`: occupancy, the operational model, Schild and Cheng–Prusoff, binding kinetics and the one-compartment oral model for the explorers; `src/pharmacokinetics.js` counts the interaction matrices and CiPA margins; `src/trials.js` folds registrations into phase bands, computes design and enrollment shares and turns submission times into Kaplan–Meier curves; `src/manufacturing.js` stacks recalls by class and drug, counts intake limits by potency category and lays out the shortage list and the elemental impurity limits; `src/approvals.js` stacks novel approvals by kind, shares out priority and orphan approvals, bins the FDA–EMA gap and counts biosimilars by molecule.
- `src/facts.js`: sources, landmark molecules, decoder examples and the fixed tables, such as genetic support from Minikel et al. 2024, development success rates from Wong, Siah and Lo 2019, and the ICH Q3C solvent and Q3D elemental impurity limits.
- `src/charts.js`: the SVG chart engine, including hexagon maps, box plots, molecule drawings, an icicle, grids, the kinome matrix, the network, forest plots and scatter plots. `src/stats.js`: quantiles, binning, Lorenz curves, kernel densities, Kaplan–Meier curves, disproportionality and agreement statistics.
- `scripts/snapshot.mjs`: the weekly data pull. `scripts/export.mjs` writes the snapshot out as `snapshot.json`, which the published site links for download.
- `og.png`: the preview image shown when the link is shared.

## Reading the numbers

An approved drug is a parent compound that ChEMBL lists at maximum phase 4, approved in at least one jurisdiction, with its salts and hydrates folded in. First approval years are the earliest approval ChEMBL records: for medicines older than modern registration they come later than first use, and Drugs@FDA dates every application approved before 1 January 1982 to that day, which ChEMBL inherits.

Targets are the ones ChEMBL’s mechanisms of action name for approved drugs. A drug–target pair is one drug on one target, so a drug acting on three targets makes three pairs; each caption says whether it counts drugs, targets or pairs. Protein classes come from ChEMBL’s protein classification, taking the deepest class among a target’s components; mRNA, ribosome and other non-protein targets are filed by kind. Genes of drug targets are those of human single-protein and protein-complex targets, matched to HGNC by UniProt accession, because protein families and groups list every member. The genetic support figure is a fixed table from Minikel et al. 2024 and is not refreshed weekly. Kinome selectivity is S(3 µM), the share of wild-type kinase constructs bound with Kd below 3 µM, and the Gini coefficient of each inhibitor’s affinities.

Counts of ChEMBL activity records come from the Elasticsearch index behind ChEMBL’s web interface, which aggregates all 24 million records in one request; the per-document pulls for Davis 2011, Karaman 2008, Klaeger 2017 and K4DD use the same index. Davis 2011 repeats most of the Kd values it shares with Karaman 2008, so the agreement figure pairs Davis with the kinobeads study instead, an independent method. NCI-ALMANAC scores are NCI’s own: each combination well against the Bliss expectation from single-drug wells on the same plate, averaged by cell line and then across cell lines. The explorers compute their curves in the page and fetch nothing. Human PK values are the median, for each approved drug, of the oral bioavailability, half-life and plasma protein binding ChEMBL abstracts from the literature for whole-body human studies; they come from many studies and populations, so read them as typical values. CiPA margins divide each IC50 by the free therapeutic Cmax in FDA’s CiPA repository, pinned to one commit.

Trial counts come from the ClinicalTrials.gov API, one count per request. A drug trial is an interventional study with a drug or biologic intervention; phases are as registered, with phase 1/2 and 2/3 trials kept apart in the design grids and folded into phase 2 and phase 3 in the registrations chart. The reporting cohort is interventional phase 2 to 4 trials flagged as studying an FDA-regulated drug or biologic whose actual primary completion fell in 2018 to 2021. A results submission counts in the month it falls in, so the 12-month mark is a year to the day; trials without results are censored at the snapshot date, and trials that filed a certification or extension request to delay form their own curve. The flag arrived with the final rule of 42 CFR Part 11, and most drug trials in the window registered before it took effect on 18 January 2017 never set it, so they are left out. A psychedelic or cannabinoid trial counts when a drug, biologic or supplement intervention carries the compound’s name, another name or a code name; DMT leaves out multiple sclerosis trials, where the letters stand for disease-modifying therapy, and ketamine counts in trials of depression. Development success rates and R&D costs are fixed tables from Wong, Siah and Lo 2019, Wouters et al. 2020 and DiMasi et al. 2016, and are not refreshed weekly.

A recall here is one product in an openFDA enforcement report, so a single recall event can count many times; years are when FDA’s weekly report listed it. Causes are read from the reason FDA records, first from the category that opens it, such as “Lack of Assurance of Sterility” or “CGMP Deviations”, then from the rest of the text; any reason that names a nitrosamine counts as a nitrosamine recall, whatever its category. Shortages are FDA’s list as openFDA serves it: one listing per package, folded here into one entry per drug name, current if any of its packages is. The Inactive Ingredient Database is read from the newest quarterly file on FDA’s download page. The solvent and elemental impurity limits are fixed tables typed from ICH Q3C(R8) and Q3D(R2).

A novel approval is the first approval of an NDA that Drugs@FDA classes as chemical type 1, a new molecular entity, alone or with type 4, or of a BLA of type 1; an antibody is a BLA whose ingredient is named for -mab. Drugs@FDA counts a few approvals that CDER’s own lists leave out, so a year’s total can run one or two above CDER’s. Priority review and orphan designation come from Drugs@FDA’s review priority and submission properties. FDA and EMA approvals are paired by substance name, lower-cased with salts, hydrates and four-letter biologic suffixes dropped, against EMA’s first centralised authorisation of a substance that is neither a generic nor a biosimilar. Generic approvals are approved original ANDAs; equivalence codes, patents and exclusivities come from the Orange Book data files, with each patent counted once per application and pediatric extensions folded into their patent. Biosimilars are FDA’s own list, which dates some approvals to the month only.

Molecular weight, ALogP, polar surface area, hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, rotatable bonds, rule-of-five violations and QED are the properties ChEMBL calculates. Rings, ring systems, Fsp3 and elements are computed here from ChEMBL’s 2D molfiles: rings are the smallest set of smallest rings; a ring is aromatic when its π electrons number 4n + 2, counting double bonds shared across a fused system; ring systems join rings that share an atom and keep exocyclic double-bonded atoms, as Bemis–Murcko frameworks do. Stereocentres are counted from ChEMBL’s canonical SMILES.
