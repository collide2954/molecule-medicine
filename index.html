How to read these numbers

  • Rebuilt weekly. Every count comes from the files and APIs that ChEMBL, FDA, CMS, CDC, NIH and CPIC publish, pulled every Sunday. A source that fails keeps last week’s numbers and its own date. Tap a mark for its numbers.
  • Two meanings of approved. Chemistry and targets count the drugs ChEMBL lists at maximum phase 4, approved somewhere, one entry per parent compound with its salts folded in. Regulatory history counts FDA novel approvals. Each caption says which.
  • First approval dates. ChEMBL dates a drug by the earliest approval it records, so medicines older than modern registration, such as aspirin and morphine, carry later years than their first use.
  • Potency and concentration. Affinities are plotted as pKi, pIC50 or pEC50, minus the log of the molar value, with nanomolar in the popups; plasma levels are in ng/mL.
  • Log scales. Charts that span several orders of magnitude say log scale in their caption.
  • Every figure’s numbers. Each caption ends with a table and a CSV of exactly what is drawn.
